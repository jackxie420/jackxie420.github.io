Overview

Prosody (pitch, loudness, and timing) carries emphasis, emotion, and intent beyond the words themselves. Expressive speech-to-speech translation systems try to carry it across languages, but little is known about how prosody actually corresponds between languages.

We present the first fine-grained, word-level analysis of cross-lingual prosody, using professionally dubbed TV speech in English–German, English–Spanish, and English–French. Dubbing actors aim to preserve the expressiveness of the original, which makes dubbing a natural parallel corpus for prosody.

How it works

Mine parallel speech and keep only clean pairs

Source and dubbed clips are mined with SpeechVecAlign, then filtered for audio quality, single speakers, and semantic match. About 1 in 6 pairs survives (see the funnel below).

SpeechVecAlignSQUIM (SI-SDR, STOI, PESQ)pyannoteSONAR

Word timings and cross-lingual word alignment

whisper-timestamped gives word-level timestamps. FastAlign, run in both directions and augmented with OPUS TED2013, links each word to its translation, reaching 82.2% precision on a gold German–English benchmark.

whisper-timestampedFastAlignOPUS TED2013

Frame-level pitch and energy

Pitch (F0) is extracted with pYAAPT and interpolated through unvoiced regions; energy is extracted with librosa. Clips where the pitch tracker fails are excluded.

pYAAPTlibrosa

Compare words with their translations, not their positions

Word-level segments are grouped into alignment clusters and reordered so translated words line up, even when word order differs. Each word is resampled to 20 frames, and each contour is normalized by the speaker's mean.

alignment clusters20 frames / wordmean normalization

Measure correspondence against a random baseline

Each pair is scored with Spearman rank correlation and compared with a baseline that shuffles the target contour. Leave-one-out ablations by part of speech show which words carry the shared prosody.

Findings

Finding 1

Prosody partly survives translation

Source and dubbed contours are positively correlated in every language pair, far above the shuffled baseline. Pitch corresponds more strongly than energy.

Finding 2

Nouns carry the shared prosody

Removing a word class from the contour shows how much it contributes. Nouns help most in every language pair. Function words make energy correspondence worse.

Helps correspondenceHurts correspondence
Finding 3

Timing is almost fully preserved

At the utterance level, duration and phoneme count are strongly correlated across languages (ρ ≈ 0.87–0.89). Dubbing actors rarely need to change their speaking rate much to fit the timing.
